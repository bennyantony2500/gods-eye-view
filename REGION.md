# Region Focus — Indian Ocean Theatre

This build points at **India, Pakistan, Afghanistan and China** on land, and the
**entire Indian Ocean** at sea.

It is a **focus, not a geofence**. The camera still pans anywhere, every layer
still renders anywhere, and nothing filters contacts by position. What the region
changes is where the app starts, which presets it offers, and how wide a net the
upstream proxies cast — so the feeds spend their quota on the area of interest
instead of on the whole planet.

Everything below is driven by one file: [`src/regionFocus.js`](src/regionFocus.js).

---

## The bounds

| Box | Extent | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `COUNTRY_BOUNDS` | Per country: India `6.5–35.7N, 68–97.5E` · Pakistan `23.6–37.1N, 60.8–77.9E` · Afghanistan `29.3–38.5N, 60.4–74.9E` · China `17.9–53.6N, 73.4–135.1E` | `countriesAt()`, land membership tests |
| `LAND_FOCUS_BOUNDS` | `6–54.1N, 59.9–135.6E` (the four unioned, +0.5° margin) | Overpass-scale queries, the land AOI |
| `INDIAN_OCEAN_BOUNDS` | `60S–31N, 20–147E` | The ocean AOI |
| `FLIGHT_FOCUS_BOUNDS` | `1S–54.1N, 32–135.6E` (land + northern ocean) | OpenSky bbox |
| `SURFACE_FOCUS_BOUNDS` | `60S–54.1N, 20–147E` (land + whole ocean) | AISStream, FIRMS |

The ocean box follows the IHO limits: **20°E** is the Cape Agulhas meridian (the
Atlantic boundary), **147°E** the South East Cape / Tasmania meridian (the
Pacific boundary south of Australia), **60°S** the Southern Ocean boundary, and
**31°N** takes in the heads of the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Suez.

These are axis-aligned boxes, so they necessarily include neighbouring
territory and each other — the country boxes all overlap around the Karakoram.
`countriesAt()` returns an **array** for exactly that reason: it answers "which
envelopes contain this point", never "which country is this". Anything that
needs real borders should use the Natural Earth polygons in
`src/data/local_data/natural_earth/`.

`INDIAN_OCEAN_SUBREGIONS` splits the ocean into nine named basins (Arabian Sea,
Bay of Bengal, Persian Gulf & Gulf of Oman, Red Sea & Gulf of Aden, Andaman Sea
& Malacca approaches, Mozambique Channel, Central Indian Basin, South Indian
Ocean, West Australian Coast) for trimming the AIS subscription.

## What changed

### Feeds — three proxies now bounded

| Feed | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **OpenSky** aircraft | `/states/all?extended=1` worldwide | same, plus `lamin/lomin/lamax/lomax` from `FLIGHT_FOCUS_BOUNDS` |
| **NASA FIRMS** fires | `area/csv/{key}/{source}/world/2` | `world` replaced by the theatre's `west,south,east,north` |
| **AISStream** vessels | subscribes to `[[[-90,-180],[90,180]]]` | subscribes to the theatre box |

On OpenSky credits: a bounded call is never *more* expensive than the worldwide
one, but this box is large, so don't expect the cheapest tier. The real win is
payload — a regional snapshot is a fraction of the worldwide one to transfer,
parse and render. Shrink `FLIGHT_FOCUS_BOUNDS` if credits matter more than
coverage.

Everything else was already camera-driven and needed no change: Overpass road
geometry, TomTom tiles, Nominatim, Open-Meteo, Radio Browser, terrain heights,
the regional briefing, and the `adsb.lol` military/fallback layers all query
around wherever you are looking.

### Presets — the location bar

Ten new rows lead the list, then the eight upstream cities follow unchanged:

- **Cities** (5 POIs each): New Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Islamabad, Karachi,
  Kabul, Beijing, Shanghai
- **Indian Ocean** — Strait of Hormuz, Strait of Malacca, Bab-el-Mandeb, Six
  Degree Channel, Mozambique Channel Narrows
- **Theatre Ports** — Gwadar, Colombo, Port Blair, Singapore, Djibouti

Five POIs per row is not arbitrary: the location bar binds POIs to **Q/W/E/R/T**,
so a sixth entry would render but never get a keyboard shortcut. The maritime
rows are generated by `maritimePreset()` from `MARITIME_WAYPOINTS`, so the
bounds, the feeds and the presets cannot drift apart.

All of them are searchable and voice-navigable through the existing machinery —
"take me to the Strait of Hormuz" resolves to the curated camera pose rather
than a generic geocode.

### Boot camera

`flyToAustin()` is now `flyToRegionHome()`, reading `DEFAULT_FOCUS` (New Delhi).
Same cinematic pattern: snap to 25 km looking straight down, pause 500 ms so the
first tiles arrive, then descend to a 900 m oblique.

## Turning it off

```bash
GEV_REGION_FOCUS=off npm run dev     # also: 0, false, no, global, world
```

That restores the stock worldwide OpenSky, FIRMS and AIS queries without
touching code. The presets and boot camera stay regional. `npm run doctor`
prints the active state on its `Region:` line.

## Trimming the vessel firehose

The whole Indian Ocean as one AIS subscription is a lot of vessels — it carries
Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb and Malacca. To subscribe to single basins instead:

```bash
# Arabian Sea + Bay of Bengal only
AISSTREAM_BOUNDING_BOXES=[[[8,55],[25.5,78]],[[5,78],[23,95]]]
```

**One catch worth knowing.** Setting `AISSTREAM_BOUNDING_BOXES` marks the
subscription "custom", which *disarms* the silence watchdog — a narrow filter
can be legitimately quiet for minutes, so the watchdog refuses to guess. Opt
back in with a timeout sized for your filter:

```bash
AISSTREAM_SILENCE_TIMEOUT_MS=300000
```

The built-in theatre box keeps the watchdog armed, because two minutes of total
silence across three of the world's busiest straits is a broken feed, not a
quiet patch of sea.

## Known gap: CCTV

**No public traffic-camera catalog for these four countries is wired into the
proxy.** The built-in providers are Austin Open Data, Caltrans and TfL London —
all of which stay available, and all of which are on the other side of the
planet from this region.

What ships instead is a **pilot pack** at
[`config/cctv_sources.indian-ocean.json`](config/cctv_sources.indian-ocean.json):
12 cameras with **real, hand-authored poses** — position, heading, pitch, FOV,
mount height, ground elevation — across Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Karachi,
Islamabad, Kabul, Beijing, Shanghai, Colombo and Singapore, carrying the same
public demo MP4s the project's own Shinjuku pilot pack uses.

```bash
CCTV_SOURCES_FILE=config/cctv_sources.indian-ocean.json npm run dev
```

The **geometry is real and the imagery is placeholder**. That is enough to
exercise the projection, viewshed and camera-handoff pipeline over this region,
and it is a template to drop authorised feed URLs into — swap each entry's
`url`, keep the pose. Every entry's `license` field says so explicitly. Do not
mistake a pilot camera for a live one.

## Licensing, for this region specifically

The upstream terms in [DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md) all still apply, and two
matter more here than they do in the stock build:

- **OpenSky is non-commercial**, and operational REST use in a live product can
  require a prior written agreement — even for non-profit or government use.
- **TeleGeography's submarine cable data is CC BY-NC-SA.** Indian Ocean cables
  are some of the most interesting content in that layer for this theatre, and
  it must be removed for commercial use.

Also note that **Google Maps content may not be cached, stored or rehosted** —
relevant if you extend the regional briefing or place search.
